# Hosting research (P1-T8, 2026-11-08)

Four researchers, each checked by a second one. Every claim carries its source and date.
Prices exclude GST unless stated. Decisions taken from this: ADR-011.

## AWS in India (incl. "AWS Delhi")

AWS has no full region in Delhi. What exists there is a Local Zone, ap-south-1-del-1 (zone ap-south-1-del-1a), and its parent region is Mumbai. It is small: EC2 (T3/C5/M5/R5/G4dn in a few sizes only), EBS gp2 only, ALB, ECS/EKS, VPC, Shield Standard and Direct Connect. It has no S3 and no RDS. It has no Graviton (t4g/m7g) and no t3.large, so the smallest instance with at least 8 GB is t3.xlarge (4 vCPU/16 GB) at $0.2419/h, about $177 (~₹16,600) a month, and disk is gp2 at $0.262/GB-month. The two full India regions, Mumbai (ap-south-1) and Hyderabad (ap-south-2), price the target sizes identically. Per month on-demand (730 h, at ₹93.92/USD from the ECB rate for 2026-11-06): t4g.large $32.70 (~₹3,070), m7g.large $42.56 (~₹4,000), t3.large $65.41 (~₹6,140), m7i-flex.large $73.55 (~₹6,910). Other charges: gp3 $0.0912/GB-month (100 GB = $9.12, ~₹857); S3 Standard $0.025/GB-month (~₹2.35); public IPv4 $0.005/h (~₹343/month); Route 53 hosted zone $0.50/month for each of the first 25 zones; SES runs in both regions at $0.10 per 1,000 emails; internet data transfer out is $0.1093/GB after 100 GB/month free (that allowance is shared across all regions); Hyderabad to Mumbai costs $0.086/GB. A staging VM in Mumbai or Hyderabad (t4g.large + 100 GB gp3 + 1 IPv4) comes to about $45.5, roughly ₹4,270 a month before 18% GST, or about ₹5,040 with it. S3 Object Lock COMPLIANCE mode is documented as a standard S3 feature; the S3 price list covers both Mumbai and Hyderabad, but I did not find a per-region Object Lock availability statement. The new Free plan (accounts created on or after 2025-07-15) gives $100 at sign-up plus up to $100 more earned through activities. It lasts 6 months or until the credits run out, whichever comes first, and works in all regions except GovCloud and China. Eligible EC2 types are t3.micro/small, t4g.micro/small, c7i-flex.large and m7i-flex.large; m7i-flex.large (2 vCPU/8 GB) is the only one that fits the 8 GB staging need. When the plan ends the account closes and data is deleted after 90 days, which conflicts with COMPLIANCE-mode WORM (AWS says the only way to remove compliance-locked objects early is to delete the account). Indian billing addresses are billed in INR by Amazon Web Services India Pvt Ltd (AWS India). AWS does not publish an INR price list; the INR figures here are my own conversions. I found no AWS sovereign cloud or separate partition for India. The only AWS sovereign partition is the European Sovereign Cloud (Germany, live January 2026). India news is about capacity: about $48B total Amazon India investment for 2026-2030, over $21B of it for cloud and AI, and a new Hyderabad data centre. Not confirmed: Local Zone use under the Free plan, Delhi LZ-to-Mumbai transfer pricing, SES free-tier terms under the new plan, and an official per-region Object Lock list. All prices come from the AWS public price-list JSON files that feed aws.amazon.com pricing pages, published 2026-11-03 to 2026-11-07.

## Indian cloud providers

Indian cloud alternatives for IndieHR staging (4 vCPU / 8 GB / 100 GB), checked 2026-11-08. Only three of the 13 providers sell low-cost, self-service VMs with published INR prices: E2E Networks, Utho and Yotta Yntraa. The rest (CtrlS/Cloud4C, Sify CloudInfinit, ESDS eNlight, NxtGen, Airtel Cloud, Jio Cloud, Tata Communications Vayu (formerly IZO), RackBank, NTT/Netmagic, Web Werks) are sales-led or quote-only, or I could not find a published price. Tata's MeitY page does link to a self-service customer-portal sign-up and a cost calculator, but I found no rate card.

THE BIG GAP: none of the Indian providers is confirmed to offer S3 Object Lock in COMPLIANCE mode.
- E2E's own docs (updated 2 Nov 2026) say Governance is "the only lock mode available today", plus Legal Hold. The bucket owner can shorten or clear a Governance lock, so it does not meet the audit-fingerprint requirement.
- Yotta's object storage page only mentions optional "immutability". Utho's documents nothing.
- Options: get written confirmation of COMPLIANCE mode from Yotta or Utho, keep only the small audit-anchor bucket on a provider that is documented to support COMPLIANCE mode, or self-host an S3 server that implements it. Whether AWS S3 Mumbai/Hyderabad supports it was not re-checked in this run, and neither was any self-hosted option.

DNS-01: lego v5.6.0 (released 2026-10-28; 236 DNS providers) has no provider for any of the 13 Indian clouds.
- E2E has a DNS API (it runs nameservers ns50/ns51.e2enetworks.net.in). Utho's DNS API is shown by its Terraform resources. Yotta sells DNS at ₹100 per domain.
- Traefik/lego would therefore need one of two workarounds. One is to delegate `_acme-challenge` with a CNAME to a lego-supported server, such as a self-hosted acme-dns (keeps it provider-neutral). The other is lego's `exec`/`httpreq` provider with a small script that calls the provider's API.

"AWS Delhi" is not a full region. It is a Local Zone (ap-south-1-del-1, launched October 2022) whose parent region is Mumbai. I could not confirm that S3 is offered there.

## Zero-cost trial options

ZERO-COST TRIAL OPTIONS WITH DATA IN INDIA (checked 2026-11-08; USD shown with INR at about ₹95/USD. That rate is indicative only: the latest dated quotes I found were ₹93–97 per USD in Apr–Jul 2026, and I could not confirm a November 2026 rate.)

BOTTOM LINE
Only one option is free with no time limit and keeps compute and disk in India: an Oracle Cloud (OCI) Always Free account with its home region set to Hyderabad (ap-hyderabad-1) or Mumbai (ap-mumbai-1).
- Oracle cut the Ampere A1 allowance from 4 OCPU / 24 GB to 2 OCPU / 12 GB from 15 June 2026. It still covers the ~8 GB RAM and 100 GB disk staging needs, but gives 2 OCPUs instead of 4, on ARM (arm64 images needed).
- It includes 200 GB of block storage and 20 GB of object storage.
- Everything else is either time-limited credit or has no India region:
  - Time-limited credit with Indian regions: Azure $200 for 30 days; GCP $300 for 90 days (Mumbai and Delhi); AWS $100 plus up to $100 for at most 6 months (Mumbai or Hyderabad); Akamai/Linode $100 (Mumbai and Chennai). Vultr's offers are inconsistent, and DigitalOcean now appears to give $5, not $200.
  - No India region for containers or a database: Render, Koyeb (no free plan for new users since Feb 2026) and Fly.io (dropped Mumbai for new machines on 30 Sep 2026; only a $1 trial).
- Vercel, Netlify and Cloudflare Pages are good only for the static frontend bundle. Vercel can run functions in Mumbai (bom1), but its free Hobby plan is non-commercial. Netlify functions have no India region. Cloudflare R2 cannot be pinned to India.
- I found no Indian Netlify-style platform with a free tier. Utho's 30-day trial needs a card, and I could not confirm its credit amount from an official page.

OCI CAVEATS
- The home region is permanent, and Always Free compute and block storage work only there.
- A1 capacity is often exhausted. Community reports say Hyderabad is a little easier than Mumbai, and each Indian region has a single availability domain.
- Sign-up needs a phone number and a credit card. RuPay cards and most Indian debit cards are reported to fail; Visa or Mastercard credit cards work.
- Oracle can reclaim idle instances when, over 7 days, 95th-percentile CPU, network and (for A1) memory are each below 20%.
- Biggest gap for us: OCI's S3-compatible API does not list any Object Lock, retention or versioning calls. OCI's WORM feature is bucket-level retention rules, managed only through OCI's own API, console or SDKs. A locked rule cannot be removed, there is a 14-day wait before locking, and it cannot be combined with versioning. So our S3 Object Lock COMPLIANCE audit-fingerprint code will not work on OCI without an adapter.
  - Akamai Object Storage does support S3 Object Lock in compliance mode, but I could not confirm that for its India regions.
  - AWS S3 in Mumbai would work while the AWS credits last.
- Daily backups to a second Indian location cannot be free on OCI, because Always Free storage is home-region only.

TUNNELS FROM A LOCAL MACHINE
- Tailscale Funnel: TLS ends on our own machine and the relay cannot read the traffic. But it only offers *.ts.net names (no custom domain), only ports 443/8443/10000, it is in beta, and the free Personal plan is non-commercial.
- ngrok free: has a Mumbai point of presence, but limits are 1 GB out and 20k requests a month, visitors see a warning page first, and there are no custom domains.
- Cloudflare Tunnel: free, but traffic on Cloudflare-proxied names is decrypted at Cloudflare's edge, which can be any data centre. Cloudflare's India-only option (Regional Services) is an Enterprise add-on.
- In any case, this 6.7 GB dev machine cannot run the full stack.

RECOMMENDATION FOR A SYNTHETIC-DATA DEMO
1. Open one OCI Always Free account with Hyderabad as the home region (the owner signs up with a Visa or Mastercard credit card).
2. Run the existing Docker Compose staging stack (Traefik, Django, Postgres, Redis, Keycloak, nginx frontend) on a single A1.Flex VM with 2 OCPU / 12 GB and a 100–150 GB boot or block volume.
3. Get the *.app.amhcloud.in wildcard certificate with Let's Encrypt DNS-01 using any DNS provider Traefik supports. Keep DNS-only (no proxying) so TLS ends on the India VM.
4. For the audit fingerprints in the demo, either:
   - add an OCI retention-rule adapter, or
   - run SeaweedFS (which documents S3 Object Lock) on the VM, clearly labelled as not real WORM.
   Do not use MinIO: its community edition was archived in 2026. Move to a real compliance-mode store (AWS S3 Mumbai, Akamai, or OCI native) before any real data.
5. Use the 30–90-day credits (GCP Mumbai or Delhi, Azure Central India, AWS Mumbai or Hyderabad) only for short tests: second-location backup drills, or the 4 vCPU size.

On "AWS Delhi": AWS has no Delhi region, only a Local Zone (ap-south-1-del-1a) attached to the Mumbai region. AWS's full India regions are Mumbai (ap-south-1) and Hyderabad (ap-south-2).

## Portability tooling

Research date 2026-11-08. Nothing was signed up for and no forms were filled in.

OpenTofu: the latest stable release is v1.13.9 (05-11-2026, MPL-2.0). AWS, Azure, GCP, OCI, DigitalOcean, Vultr and Linode all have current providers in the OpenTofu registry, each updated within the last month. E2E Networks has a provider (e2eterraformprovider/e2e 2.2.12, 31-08-2026) on the Terraform registry and in the OpenTofu registry's docs API. It covers nodes (with a start_script and location "Delhi"), object stores and VPCs, but has no DNS resource. Utho has a provider (uthoplatforms/utho 0.7.1, 19-10-2026) on the Terraform registry, with DNS and object-storage resources. However, it is not in the OpenTofu registry, and its README names a different namespace that returns 404. Yotta (Yntraa) says its VMs work with Terraform, but no public provider was found.

cloud-init: AWS, Azure, GCP, OCI, DigitalOcean, Vultr and Linode/Akamai support it officially. E2E only offers a 2,048-character "start script" that runs on every boot, and the docs do not say it is cloud-init. Utho's provider docs show no user_data argument. Yotta could not be confirmed.

ACME DNS-01: Traefik v3.7 uses lego (v5.6.0 is the latest, 28-10-2026). lego supports Route 53 (route53), OCI DNS (oraclecloud), Cloudflare, DigitalOcean, Vultr, Linode, deSEC (desec), PowerDNS (pdns) and RFC2136 (dnsupdate). It also has acmedns, exec and httpreq. It does not support E2E or Utho, so those need CNAME delegation of _acme-challenge to a supported DNS service.

WORM object storage: Garage cannot do Object Lock (an architectural limit in its own docs). SeaweedFS (4.52, 02-11-2026) implements COMPLIANCE mode and is still fixing it actively, so test it before relying on it. Ceph RGW supports GOVERNANCE and COMPLIANCE but has had bypass bugs, and it is heavy to run. MinIO Community Edition went into maintenance mode in Dec 2025 and was archived in 2026; only the commercial AIStor line continues. A finding from outside the brief: E2E's own object storage offers only Governance lock and Legal Hold, with no COMPLIANCE mode, so it is not enough for the audit-fingerprint bucket.

Backups and testing: pgBackRest v2.60.0 (18-10-2026) works with any S3-compatible endpoint through repo-s3-endpoint and repo-s3-uri-style=path, but it does not manage Object Lock itself. Pebble v2.10.1 (20-04-2026, MPL-2.0) can test Traefik locally (caServer plus LEGO_CA_CERTIFICATES), with DNS-01 through a local PowerDNS or RFC2136 server.

AWS has no Delhi region. Delhi is a Local Zone (ap-south-1-del-1a) under Mumbai (ap-south-1), and it lacks services such as RDS. The full AWS regions in India are Mumbai and Hyderabad (ap-south-2).

Recommended layout: one provider-neutral Ubuntu bootstrap script, plus four small swappable OpenTofu modules (vm, dns, objectstore, smtp-credentials) behind fixed inputs and outputs. ACME challenges go to one dedicated DNS zone that is independent of the VM provider. All backups and anchors use the generic S3 API. The WORM bucket is kept in a different provider account or trust domain from the app VM. OpenTofu state encryption is on, and state is kept in an Indian S3-compatible bucket.
