# Rewrite the Hospital Blueprint for SaaS

The open-source blueprint is a credible parts list for one self-hosted tertiary hospital, but it is not yet a blueprint for a commercial multi-tenant SaaS, and more than thirty of its specific claims are wrong, outdated or unverifiable as of 2 November 2026. The strongest clinical foundation is **CARE (MIT)**, whose data model is natively FHIR-shaped and covers OPD, IPD, ICU, pharmacy, inventory and GST billing ([GitHub](https://github.com/ohcnetwork/care)). It comes with two catches: its tenant model is an organisation hierarchy rather than hard isolation, and its production ABDM, Scribe and HCX plugins are no longer publicly visible, so M1–M3 must be built or licensed. Ozone is now Odoo-based rather than ERPNext-based, and it is heavy and single-tenant, while Bahmni runs about 30 containers per deployment. Several licence traps sit inside the "unrestricted" column: Orthanc's PostgreSQL, DICOMweb, authorization and Stone plugins are AGPL, Synapse is AGPL rather than Apache, Stalwart's multi-tenancy is paid, and MinIO's community repository was archived in April 2026. ERPNext v16 supports only MariaDB, not PostgreSQL. The regulatory clock is concrete: DPDP's core duties start around mid-May 2027, ABDM incentives now require v3 APIs and M3, NABH certifies HIS/EMR vendors, and CDSCO now regulates software with a medical purpose. The market is quote-only and fragmented, which leaves room for transparent per-bed pricing in 20–100 bed Tier 2/3 hospitals, but free government and open-source alternatives cap what core EMR functions can charge.

## Licences decide where proprietary code may live

For a hosted product, the useful question is not "is it open source" but "what duty does serving it over a network create". The blueprint's licence-governance table labels nearly everything "Unrestricted", which holds for self-hosting one's own hospital but not for a vendor selling a service.

Permissive components are the safe place to modify and embed. CARE (MIT) permits closed proprietary extensions, and its plugin system, in which Django apps are declared in `plug_config.py` and frontend micro-frontends load through Module Federation, lets proprietary modules sit outside the core repository ([pluggable-apps doc](https://github.com/ohcnetwork/care/blob/develop/docs/development/pluggable-apps.md)). Keycloak (Apache-2.0), OHIF (MIT), Frappe Framework (MIT), HAPI FHIR (Apache-2.0), Zulip (Apache-2.0), the official MCP SDK (MIT) and FastMCP (Apache-2.0) fall in the same bucket ([Keycloak](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak); [OHIF LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OHIF/Viewers/master/LICENSE); [frappe LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/frappe/frappe/develop/LICENSE)). Open-core carve-outs still bite inside permissive repositories: Chatwoot's `enterprise/` directory needs a paid subscription for production use, Novu's `ee/` folders are commercial, and Stalwart's Enterprise tier gates the multi-tenancy, per-domain directories and SCIM that a multi-hospital mail service needs ([Chatwoot enterprise licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chatwoot/chatwoot/HEAD/enterprise/LICENSE); [Stalwart compare](https://stalw.art/compare/)).

GPL components impose no source duty on a vendor that only hosts them, but they do on one that ships on-premise installers or images. ERPNext, HRMS, India Compliance, India Payroll, Marley Health and Orthanc's core are GPL, and Frappe's licence page confirms ERPNext GPLv3 and the framework MIT ([Frappe licence page](https://docs.frappe.io/legal/others/license-and-trademark.md)). Because many Indian hospital buyers ask for on-premise or hybrid deployments, the sales model decides whether GPL bites. Whether a Frappe app that extends ERPNext DocTypes counts as a derivative work is unresolved, and no maintainer answered the question when it was asked on the forum ([forum](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/using-erpnext-for-maintaining-ledgers-and-accounts-in-e-commerce-saas/120840)). The safer pattern is proprietary logic in separate services that reach ERPNext through REST and webhooks. The ERPNext name and logo cannot appear in a product, company or domain name without written permission from Frappe ([licence and trademark](https://docs.frappe.io/legal/others/license-and-trademark.md)).

AGPL is the real constraint. Frappe's Helpdesk, CRM, Insights and Press are AGPL-3.0; Nextcloud, Synapse, Dendrite, Grafana, Loki and ONLYOFFICE are AGPL; and Orthanc's PostgreSQL, DICOMweb, object-storage, authorization, Python and Stone components are AGPLv3 with no permissive option because they combine with a GPL core ([Orthanc licensing FAQ](https://orthanc.uclouvain.be/book/faq/licensing.html)). Orthanc's own FAQ spells out the safe boundary: calling Orthanc over REST or DICOM from a proprietary system is fine, but deriving from an AGPL plugin or viewer on a proprietary cloud platform is "restricted". In practice the PostgreSQL index plugin is mandatory for any scaled Orthanc, so a PACS-backed SaaS runs AGPL code in production and must keep it unmodified or publish changes. MPL-2.0 components (OpenMRS, OpenELIS, OIE, Ozone FOSS, Collabora's source) are file-level copyleft and are not normally triggered by hosting ([OpenMRS LICENSE](https://github.com/openmrs/openmrs-core/blob/master/LICENSE)). Two further wrinkles matter: BridgeLink's WebAdmin is under Business Source License 1.1 even though its engine is MPL-2.0 ([BridgeLink](https://github.com/Innovar-Healthcare/BridgeLink)), and ONLYOFFICE's 2026 licence adds an attribution term for modified versions ([IT-Connect](https://www.it-connect.tech/onlyoffice-docs-9-4-drops-the-20-connection-limit-and-updates-its-open-source-license/)).

Model licences add a second layer. Gemma 4 moved to Apache-2.0 in March 2026 ([Google Open Source Blog](https://opensource.googleblog.com/2026/03/gemma-4-expanding-the-gemmaverse-with-apache-20.html)), and Qwen3.8-27B, gpt-oss, Mistral Small 4 and the Sarvam models are Apache-2.0 on Hugging Face ([Qwen3.8-27B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B); [sarvam-30b](https://huggingface.co/api/models/sarvamai/sarvam-30b)). MedGemma is the exception for medical work: its Health AI Developer Foundations terms require flow-down of restrictions, tell distributors to seek regulatory authorisation where applicable, and forbid uses that would make Google a device "manufacturer" ([HAI-DEF terms](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/terms)). Custom licences also govern Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Mistral Medium and Large, and Meta's Muse-Spark-Open ([HF listings](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=mistralai&sort=createdAt&direction=-1&limit=15)). Finally, clinical content has its own licences: DrugBank is non-commercial without a separate agreement and NLM retired its RxNav interaction API in January 2024, so no open, commercially usable, severity-graded interaction dataset exists ([DrugBank FAQ](https://www.drugbank.ca/faqs); [DrugBank blog](https://blog.drugbank.com/nih-discontinues-their-drug-interaction-api/)). SNOMED CT is free in India but needs an affiliate licence through the national release centre ([SNOMED India](https://www.snomed.org/members/india)).

## Only CARE and Frappe offer a credible route to multi-tenancy

The blueprint assumes one hospital network owns its stack. A SaaS needs a tenant boundary at every layer, and the evidence shows that almost no component in the list was designed for mutually distrusting commercial tenants. The practical pattern is a shared control plane (identity, billing, ABDM bridge, observability) over tenant-isolated data planes, with isolation chosen per layer.

Frappe's site model gives the strongest built-in database isolation, and CARE's small footprint makes database-per-tenant affordable, so a CARE-plus-ERPNext SaaS can offer true per-tenant data stores at both the clinical and ledger layers. The clinical depth ranking is the reverse: CARE leads, Bahmni and Ozone follow, and Marley is shallowest, and Marley disclosed a systemic permission weakness in which only 3 of 147 whitelisted functions enforced permission checks, with fix PRs following and v16.7.0 adding related permission gating ([issue #943](https://github.com/earthians/marley/issues/943); [v16.7.0](https://github.com/earthians/marley/releases/tag/v16.7.0)). Marley is therefore the runner-up for small clinics wanting ERP-integrated billing, not a foundation for tertiary care.

Three operational risks follow from this architecture. First, Press, the open-source control plane behind Frappe Cloud, is AGPL-3.0, but its only documented setup is a staff-oriented local development guide that needs three servers and cloud accounts ([Press setup](https://docs.frappe.io/cloud/local-fc-setup)). A reseller should expect to build its own tenant-provisioning layer. Second, the India Compliance API is bundled free on Frappe Cloud, but self-hosted operators must buy credits at one credit per successful call in minimum lots of 1,000, and the per-credit price was not found ([Frappe docs](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/gst-api-credentials-and-credits)). Third, one ABDM integration needs one bridge URL with facility routing, since the HFR ID doubles as the HIP ID for each tenant ([Nirmitee](https://nirmitee.io/blog/abdm-integration-milestones-m1-m2-m3-m4-multi-software-guide/), a vendor source).

The infrastructure choices in the blueprint also need updating for multi-tenancy. CloudNativePG, with Barman-based backup and PostgreSQL 18 defaults, suits per-tenant databases better than hand-run Patroni ([CNPG releases](https://github.com/cloudnative-pg/cloudnative-pg/releases)). Ingress-nginx lost all releases and security patches in March 2026, so new clusters should use Traefik or Gateway API ([Kubernetes blog](https://www.kubernetes.io/blog/2026/01/29/ingress-nginx-statement/)). MinIO's community edition ships source only with no binaries, so object storage for DICOM and documents should be Ceph/Rook, SeaweedFS or an Indian-region managed S3 ([MinIO](https://github.com/minio/minio)). Keycloak 26.8 supports stateless mode with sessions in the database, which removes the Infinispan clustering burden ([26.8.0 release](https://www.keycloak.org/2026/10/keycloak-2680-released)).

## India's compliance clock runs through 2026 and 2027

The regulatory obligations split into what the software must do and what the vendor company must hold. ABDM integration is a commercial requirement rather than a legal mandate on private hospitals, since no primary statement making it mandatory was found, but three levers make it table stakes. NHA's Digital Health Incentive Scheme, Corrigendum 7 (9 April 2026, valid April to September 2026), pays only for records linked to KYC-verified ABHA addresses and requires **integration up to M3 for hospitals and clinics** and M2 for labs and pharmacies ([DHIS Corrigendum 7](https://rajswasthya.rajasthan.gov.in/admin/upload/letter/2026/05/40%20Dt.05.05.2026%20NHM%20CSR%20NDHM%20(ABDM)-01779-PART-14.pdf)). NABH's HIS/EMR vendor certification requires a valid M3 certificate. And PM-JAY claims through NHCX earn Rs 200 or 10% of the claim for the facility and Rs 10 per claim for the software company, capped at Rs 1 crore. The scheme is modest for vendors: through 18 June 2026 digital solution companies had received Rs 26+ crore against Rs 107+ crore to hospitals ([PIB backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jul/doc202676912801.pdf)). Whether a Corrigendum 8 extended the scheme after September 2026 is unknown.

The vendor's ABDM path runs through the sandbox: register for client credentials, register the bridge URL, build at least two milestones (a vendor-reported exit rule that another vendor guide did not confirm), pass NHA functional tests and a WASA security audit by an empanelled auditor, then complete the exit form for production credentials ([Nirmitee](https://nirmitee.io/blog/abdm-integration-milestones-m1-m2-m3-m4-multi-software-guide/)). The v3 APIs are current, and Fidelius encryption uses ECDH, HKDF-SHA256 and AES-256-GCM over Curve25519 in Weierstrass form, not the X25519 form most libraries default to ([abdm_fidelius docs](https://pub.dev/documentation/abdm_fidelius/0.1.0/)). FHIR bundles follow the NRCeS Implementation Guide v6.5.0, with a 7.0.0 build still in draft ([NRCeS IG](https://nrces.in/ndhm/fhir/r4/)). NHCX is not legally mandatory, since IRDAI circulars use language such as "may endeavor", and the last official count was 34 insurers and about 300 hospitals in July 2024 ([MoHFW](https://mohfw.gov.in/press-info/7558)). PM-JAY runs on NHA's TMS 2.0 portal with no documented HMIS API, so the claims layer needs an NHCX adapter and a portal-assist path behind one payer-gateway interface ([Nirmitee](https://nirmitee.io/blog/nhcx-pmjay-tms-ayushman-bharat-claims/), a vendor source).

DPDP drives the data-handling design. The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025. Consent Manager registration begins about 13 November 2026, and the core duties (notice, security safeguards, 72-hour breach report, retention and erasure, children's data) start about 13 May 2027, with sources disagreeing by a day or two ([ILF Law](https://www.ilflaw.com/publications/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified/); [AZB Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/)). A proposal to shorten the Significant Data Fiduciary timeline to 12 months had not been notified by October 2026 ([MirvoLegal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)). The hospital is the Data Fiduciary and the SaaS vendor is normally its Data Processor, bound by contract to the Rule 6 safeguards of encryption, access control, logging and backups, with logs kept at least one year. Clinical establishments are exempt from verifiable parental consent when processing is limited to health services. Erasure yields to retention duties ("unless law requires retention"), so clinical records survive erasure requests. Separately, CERT-In's 2022 directions require incident reports within **6 hours**, clocks synchronised to NIC or NPL, and logs kept for a rolling **180 days within Indian jurisdiction** ([CERT-In directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf)). DPDP does not mandate India hosting, but ABDM's Health Data Management Policy reportedly says personal data must not be stored outside India, a point the researchers could not confirm against the primary PDF and which secondary reports contradict ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2022/06/223-new-health-data-management-policy/)). Keep PHI, backups and logs in Indian regions: AWS Mumbai and Hyderabad, Azure's four India regions, and GCP Mumbai and Delhi all qualify, and E2E and Yotta are reported MeitY-empanelled ([PDP Spectra](https://pdpspectra.com/blog/india-cloud-adoption-aws-azure-gcp-2026); [Yotta](https://channeldrive.in/data-center/yotta-gets-empanelled-with-meity-as-accredited-csp/)).

Accreditation and clinical-governance rules shape product scope. NABH's 6th-edition hospital standards took effect on 1 January 2025 and its IMS chapter covers records, confidentiality and retention ([NABH notification](https://dev.nabh.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Notification-%E2%80%93-Implementation-Plan-of-6th-Edition-Standards.pdf)). NABH separately certifies HIS/EMR vendors under its Digital Health Standards (first edition September 2024): Basic or Advanced level, two-year validity, a prerequisite of **three live Indian hospital deployments** and an M3 certificate, with fees of Rs 50,000 to Rs 1,50,000 plus GST. A second-edition draft went out for comment on 22 July 2026 ([NABH programme page](https://nabh.co/programmes/digital-health-standards-his-emr-systems/); [public notice](https://nabh-portal-live.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/22172921/Public-Notice-for-Digital-Health-Standards_HIS-EMR-2nd-Edition.pdf)). For labs, the ISO 15189:2022 transition ended in December 2025, so NABL-accredited labs should all be on the 2022 version, interpreted in India through NABL 112 ([UKAS](https://www.ukas.com/accreditation/iso-15189-transition/)). The Clinical Establishments Rules require rate display in English and the local language, and on about 12 October 2026 the Union Health Secretary asked states to enforce it, which makes a versioned bilingual tariff master a compliance feature ([Omnicuris](https://www.omnicuris.com/medshots/daily_updates/centre-mandates-display-of-hospital-service-rates_12-Oct-26_18-11), a secondary source). The NMC's 2023 conduct regulations, with their three-year retention rule, were put in abeyance on 23 August 2023, so the 2002 MCI regulations still govern record retention ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/nmc-registered-medical-practitioner-professional-conduct-regulations-2023)). The researchers did not retrieve the 2002 text, which is widely cited as three years for indoor records, so the product should default to longer retention.

CDSCO is the new variable for the AI layer. Its Medical Device Software guidance, finalised in July 2026 as CDSCO/MD/GD/MDSW/01/2026, brings any software with a medical purpose (diagnosis, prediction, treatment decisions, AI-enabled CDS) under the Medical Devices Rules 2017 in Classes A to D. It excludes ERP, general-wellness and data-management software, and AI applicants must document training-data demographics and Indian sub-population performance ([Emergo](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/india-cdsco-finalizes-guidance-medical-device-software)). A plain HIS/EMR stays outside scope; sepsis prediction, triage scoring, dose calculators and radiology AI such as MONAI-based detection likely do not. Whether a SaaS operator counts as the "manufacturer" is unresolved, and the primary CDSCO document was not fetched. Statutory registers add module work: PCPNDT Form F with two-year retention, a Schedule H1 register kept three years, NDPS records, ART Act registry reporting and others ([CAHO PCPNDT guidance](https://caho.in/files/PCPNDT%20(1).pdf); [NHSRC](https://www.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2021-07/Attachment%20A%2C%20Schedule%20H1%20register%20and%20NDPS%20Forms.pdf)). GST rules favour clinical billing simplicity: clinical services are exempt, non-ICU rooms above Rs 5,000 per day attract 5% without input credit, and since GST 2.0 on 22 September 2025 most medicines moved from 12% to 5% ([GST Council press release](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/press_release_press_information_bureau.pdf); [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.in/en/insights/blogs/non-icu-rooms-to-be-carved-out-from-healthcare-services-under-gst)). Rates must be date-effective, and the Labour Codes in force since 21 November 2025 change PF and gratuity wage bases, so India Payroll's calculations need conformance testing ([iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/labour-codes-2026-implementation-complete-guide-to-what-is-in-force-what-changed-and-what-to-do-now/)).

## The AI layer is a generation old and a regulatory exposure

The blueprint's AI section names Llama 3.3 70B, Qwen 2.5 72B, Meditron, BioMistral, Faster-Whisper large-v3, MONAI Label and Server-Sent Events. Each is one to two generations behind. Meta's last Llama-branded open weights are Llama 4 (April 2025), with newer releases under a separate Muse brand ([HF meta-llama](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=meta-llama&sort=createdAt&direction=-1&limit=12)). Practical replacements are Gemma 4 31B or Qwen3.8-27B for single-GPU dense reasoning (roughly 14 to 16 GB of weights at 4-bit by the researchers' arithmetic, not vendor figures), gpt-oss-120b or Mistral Small 4 for larger mixture-of-experts needs, and Sarvam-30B or 105B for Indic work. MedGemma 1.5 4B is the specialist but carries the gated HAI-DEF terms, and all MedGemma versions remain Gemma-3-based. No independent clinical benchmark comparing these models was found, so an in-house evaluation on Hindi and Hinglish clinical notes is required before any claim of accuracy.

Speech is the weakest blueprint claim. Zero-shot Whisper performs poorly on Indic languages, with word error rates above 100% reported in some Hindi cases, while AI4Bharat's IndicConformer (MIT, 22 languages) reports 13.2 Hindi WER on a Vaani benchmark ([Vividh-ASR](https://huggingface.co/blog/adalat-ai/vividh-benchmark); [IndicConformer card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual)). Sarvam's Saaras v4 handles code-mixed speech and keyterm biasing but is a hosted API only. The existing CARE scribe pattern is instructive: `care_scribe` is gone, and its replacement `care_eka_scribe_fe` is a thin frontend that sends audio to Eka Care's hosted API, filling form fields from a template and requiring clinician review ([care_eka_scribe_fe](https://github.com/ohcnetwork/care_eka_scribe_fe)). The "voice-to-form with review" pattern is worth copying; the open ASR pipeline does not exist there.

MCP changed materially. Revision 2026-07-28 removes the initialize handshake and the `Mcp-Session-Id` header, makes servers stateless, deprecates HTTP+SSE, Roots, Sampling, Logging and OAuth Dynamic Client Registration, and requires RFC 9728 protected-resource metadata and RFC 8707 audience binding ([MCP changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog); [authorization spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/authorization)). Statelessness helps a multi-tenant SaaS scale behind an ordinary load balancer. The blueprint's "stdio or SSE" transport and its tool list should be rebuilt around Streamable HTTP, tenant-scoped tokens validated at the MCP server, and deterministic clinical tools, with the model only explaining outputs. Open FHIR MCP servers are weak foundations: wso2's is maintained but generic, and the-momentum's last push was October 2025 ([PyPI fhir-mcp-server](https://pypi.org/pypi/fhir-mcp-server/json); [GitHub](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=repo:the-momentum/fhir-mcp-server)).

The blueprint's "no external AI APIs" premise is no longer the only compliant option. AWS added an India-only inference profile on 29 September 2026 that routes Claude Opus 5, Sonnet 5 and Haiku 4.5 between Mumbai and Hyderabad, keeps inference in India and applies zero data retention by default ([AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/amazon-bedrock-expands-claude-model-availability-to-india-cross-region-inference/)). AWS warns residency customers to verify destination Regions rather than trust the profile prefix, and the earlier India access path routed globally. For a SaaS, a hybrid is more practical than per-hospital GPU clusters: self-hosted vLLM (Apache-2.0, 0.33.0) for tenants that demand it, and an in-India hosted option elsewhere, governed by a processor agreement. Two further blueprint items need replacing rather than patching. The "local pharmacological database" for interactions has no open source, so a licensed feed is required. The blueprint's OpenQC engine is a 32-commit, single-author, GPL-3.0, SQLite-based PCR quality-control app with zero stars, created 30 March to 1 April 2026 with an AI-agent guide in the repository ([OpenQC](https://github.com/ashuein/OpenQC)); Westgard rules are simple enough to reimplement, or OpenELIS 3.3.0's QC and release-blocking can serve, so the dependency should be dropped.

## A quote-only market leaves a pricing gap under Rs 800 per bed

Published prices in India's hospital software market are scarce, and almost every figure comes from vendor-authored blogs that rank their authors first, so treat them as indicative. Clinic EMRs sell at Rs 1,000 to 7,000 per month (MocDoc, Practo Ray, HealthPlix). Hospital-tier products quote on request, with a Codingclave comparison putting Insta by Practo near Rs 25,000 per month, Birlamedisoft at Rs 2.5 lakh one-time and Attune at Rs 5 lakh and above ([Codingclave](https://codingclave.com/blog/best-hospital-management-software-india-2026)). The same vendor's cost guide bands SaaS at Rs 2–5 lakh per year for 20–50 beds and Rs 4–12 lakh for 50–150 beds, which implies roughly Rs 330–830 per bed per month, and ABDM add-ons of Rs 50,000–2 lakh ([Codingclave guide](https://codingclave.com/guides/hospital-management-software-cost-india-2026)). Treat that as low-confidence. An add-on listing of Rs 1,499 per month per 10 beds (about Rs 150 per bed) sits at the cheap end ([SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/clinizy-care/pricing)). No major vendor publishes a per-bed hospital price.

The underserved segment is 20–100 bed private hospitals and nursing homes in Tier 2/3 cities. An ISB blog estimates about 30,000 hospitals with 30–100 beds and 45,000 with fewer than 30, and an ISB author says health software is mostly built for large corporate hospitals and is seen as too costly, feature-heavy or hard to install without support ([Newsreel Asia](https://newsreel.asia/articles/india-healthcare-small-medium-hospitals-digitalisation)). A Ken Research survey of 2,000 decision-makers (a marketing publication whose methodology cannot be verified) reports cost and ROI doubt at 68%, training resistance at 57%, poor internet or power at 49%, and module adoption of 81% for billing and 78% for registration against 42% for clinical notes ([Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/articles/clinical-digitisation-barrier-hms-vendors-india)). Together they suggest the buying order: billing and registration first, then claims, ABDM and clinical depth. Market-size estimates diverge by an order of magnitude, from IMARC's USD 739 million for India's hospital EHR market in 2025 to Ken's unexplained USD 4.08 billion for hospital information systems in 2024 ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-hospital-electronic-health-records-market)). Use IMARC as the conservative anchor.

Competition falls in three groups. Funded SaaS players are KareXpert (Reliance-backed, reporting 500+ hospitals in an undated, unverified item), Practo/Insta, HealthPlix and Eka Care; the long tail of licence vendors includes Birlamedisoft, Suvarna, Akhil, Medixcel and Lifetrenz ([Precedence Research](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/news/karexpert-global-expansion-jio); [NABH software listing](https://nabh.co/software/plus91-technologies-pvt-ltd/)). Government and open-source options anchor expectations at zero: NIC's e-Hospital for public hospitals, and Bahmni, whose model is free software with paid implementation partners and which NHA reportedly works with on a lightweight version for small facilities ([Bahmni FAQ](https://www.bahmni.org/faq); [UNDP DigitalX](https://digitalx.undp.org/bahmni_1.html)). No evidence of Epic or Oracle in Indian hospital chains was found. The lesson from KareXpert's 2021 goal of one lakh hospitals by 2026, which no evidence shows was met, is that sales cycles are slow ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2021/08/reliance-funded-startup-digitally-transforming-hospitals/amp)). A wedge built on transparent per-bed pricing, days-not-months onboarding, offline tolerance, billing and claims depth, and an implementation-partner channel for training looks stronger than competing on core EMR features.

The evidence leaves several matters open. The CARE ABDM plugin's certification status, and whether OHC will license it, could not be established. The ONLYOFFICE 9.4 licence text and Collabora's CODE cap were not read from primary sources, the DHIS position after September 2026 is unknown, and the legal question of GPL derivation for ERPNext-extending apps needs counsel. ## Conclusion

The most useful result is a reordering of the blueprint's priorities. Its centre of gravity is the clinical and imaging stack, but the commercial constraints sit in the layers beneath and beside it: AGPL plugins in the PACS path, a database mismatch between CARE (PostgreSQL) and ERPNext (MariaDB), a mail and chat tier whose licences gate multi-tenancy, and an ABDM integration whose best open-source implementation has gone private. A SaaS vendor therefore owns three assets the blueprint treats as free: an ABDM/NHCX gateway with per-tenant routing, a payer-aware tariff and package engine (no open-source Indian equivalent was found), and a tenant-provisioning control plane.

Time ordering matters as much as architecture. DPDP's core duties arrive in about six months, NABH vendor certification needs three live hospitals before it can start, and the DHIS incentive that rewards v3/M3 compliance has an unconfirmed future. That makes certified ABDM v3 plus a defensible processor posture the early gating item, with AI features added behind clinician-in-the-loop controls and a CDSCO assessment. The commercial opening is real but narrow: a hospital that wants transparent per-bed pricing and fast onboarding, served by a stack in which the vendor, not the open-source community, carries the integration, compliance and support burden.
